Project LIGHT — Application Blueprint (PRD + UX Specification)

Status: v0.1 working draft · Audience: Founder, PM, UI/UX, Frontend, Backend, QA, AI/Recsys Name: "LIGHT" is a placeholder; trademark/domain not checked.

How to read this document

  • Feature tags used throughout: [CORE] directly drives the core loop · [SUPPORT] makes the core loop safe or usable · [FUTURE] valuable later · [BLOAT] do not build.
  • Release tags: MVP (first build, ~first 6 months), V2, V3.
  • Screen IDs are stable. Use them as ticket prefixes.
  • What is assumption vs. evidence. Product structure here follows the earlier strategy blueprint. Thresholds, costs, timelines and copy are proposals to be tested, not benchmarks. Competitor statistics and legal timelines cited earlier must be verified before external use. Have counsel review age-gating, DPDP consent flows, and grievance-officer requirements before launch.
  • Deliberate deviation from the brief. You asked me not to assume the flow Interests → Communities → Interaction → Connection is correct. The research points to a refinement: the unit that produces relationships is not the large community but the small recurring Circle (4–8 people) plus Activities (real sessions). So the loop below inserts Circles and Activities between "Community" and "Connection", and Communities become lightweight local interest hubs in MVP rather than a Reddit-style feed.

PART 1 — PRODUCT FOUNDATION

1.1 Product purpose

Problem. Young urban Indians (18–29) who have moved city, graduated, started work or work remotely often lack a few nearby people to repeatedly do things with. Existing tools fail in specific ways:

Existing tool Where it fails for this job
Dating apps Match before context; conversations die; intent ambiguity; harassment before moderation
Reddit / WhatsApp groups Discovery is fragmented; no progression from "replied to my post" to "we meet weekly"
Discord Persistent but not local; no introduction mechanism
Meetup / event apps Event-centric, one-off; large anonymous groups; weak repeat-contact mechanics
Instagram Works only for existing social graphs

Job to be done: "Help me find a few people nearby whom I would actually enjoy spending time with, repeatedly, without pressure."

Product promise: Find people nearby who are into the same things — and actually do something together. Dating is one possible outcome, never the entry requirement.

1.2 Target users

Segment Initial target? Rationale
Recent city movers, 22–29 (job relocation) Yes — primary Highest felt pain, clear trigger event, strong Reddit signal
Young professionals, WFH/hybrid Yes — primary Lack of incidental social contact
Recent graduates (21–24) Yes — secondary Losing campus network
Introverted / socially anxious Served by design, not targeted by marketing Small groups + structured activities help them; do not label them
Hobby community members / hosts Yes — supply side Hosts are your distribution layer
College students (18–21) No (defer) Campus networks exist; higher safety/age-boundary risk; different acquisition
Pure dating seekers No (accept, don't target) Most competitive segment; dating is layered on later
Event-only users Accept as by-product Do not build ticketing marketplace

Beachhead cohort (proposal): 22–29, one metro (e.g., Bengaluru), 2–3 zones, 5 activity categories (badminton/sports, movies, photography, gaming/board games, food/café). Choose city based on your team's ability to recruit hosts in person.

1.3 Relationship modes

Decision: one experience, not separate modes. Separate Dating / Friendship / Community modes triple the cold-start problem and reproduce the intent confusion users complain about.

Instead, every user sets an Intent (multi-select, changeable anytime, private by default):

Intent value Meaning Effect
Friends Wants friendships Default escalation outcome = Friend
Dating Open to romantic connection Can be offered "Dating" option in escalation only when both users have it
Just activities Wants activity partners/groups only No 1:1 escalation prompts; still can be invited by others as activity buddy
Not sure yet Exploring Treated as Friends + Just activities; prompted to revisit after 3 activities

Rules: 1. Intent is never shown as a public "dating" badge in Circles. It is only revealed at the moment of mutual escalation. 2. Dating intent never enables unsolicited romantic contact. The only pathway to a 1:1 is mutual escalation (Part 15). 3. Circles may be tagged Friends-only at creation, in which case dating escalation is disabled inside that Circle (users can still connect as friends). 4. Women-only, LGBTQ+-friendly and "verified-only" Circles are first-class filters [SUPPORT].

1.4 Core product loop

Need (new city / no people)
   ↓
Pick a thing to do (Home: "This week")
   ↓
Join a Circle (4–8) or an Activity
   ↓
Group interaction (prompt + chat)
   ↓
Real-world Activity (or online-first if user prefers)
   ↓
Post-activity: "Want to see these people again?"
   ↓
Repeat with same people (Circle continues)
   ↓
Mutual connection (opt-in, 1:1)
   ↓
Friend / Activity partner / Dating (mutual)
   ↓
Creates or hosts next Activity  →  loops back

North-star metric: Meaningful Connection Rate (MCR) = activated users with a repeated positive interaction ÷ activated users. Define "repeated positive interaction" operationally in Part 42.

PART 3 — FIRST APP OPEN

AUTH-01 — Splash

  • Purpose: Brand moment while the app performs a session check. Must not exceed ~1.5s perceived.
  • Who sees it: Everyone on every cold start.
  • Entry point: App launch.
  • UI components: Logo (static; a subtle 400ms fade/scale), no tagline, no spinner unless check exceeds 1.2s (then a small progress dot).
  • Behavior: (1) Read secure-store refresh token. (2) If present, call POST /auth/refresh. (3) Route: valid session + onboarding complete → HOME-01; valid session + onboarding incomplete → resume at last completed step (server-tracked); no/invalid session → AUTH-02. (4) Also fetch remote config (min supported version, feature flags, maintenance banner).
  • Primary CTA / secondary: None.
  • User inputs: None.
  • Backend/API: POST /auth/refresh, GET /config.
  • Data collected: App version, device model/OS, install ID (no advertising ID at this stage).
  • Next screens: AUTH-02, ONB-*, HOME-01, or Force-Update screen (SYS-01) / Maintenance screen (SYS-02).
  • Success state: Routes silently.
  • Empty state: N/A.
  • Error state: Network failure with valid local token → allow cached Home in offline mode (read-only) with banner; no token + no network → AUTH-02 will show offline banner.
  • Edge cases: Token revoked (suspicious login) → AUTH-05 with "Your session ended. Please log in again"; app version below minimum → SYS-01; account suspended → SAFE-10 (account status screen); account pending deletion → SET-09 (restore option).
  • Analytics: app_opened, session_restored, session_restore_failed.
  • Release: MVP · [SUPPORT]

AUTH-02 — Welcome

  • Purpose: State the promise in one line, and get the user into sign-up with minimal friction.
  • Who sees it: Logged-out users.
  • Entry point: AUTH-01, or after logout.
  • UI components:
  • Headline: "Find your people. Do things together." (test variants: "Stop swiping. Start doing things together." / "Make a social circle in your new city.")
  • Sub-line: "Small local groups around what you enjoy."
  • 3 rotating illustration cards (Circle of 5 at a badminton court; movie night; photography walk) showing activities, not faces of models.
  • Primary button: Get started.
  • Secondary text link: I already have an account.
  • Footer: "By continuing you agree to Terms and Privacy Policy" (links). 18+ statement: "LIGHT is for people 18 and over."
  • Language toggle (English / Hinglish; more later) top right.
  • Why each element exists: One headline reduces cognitive load; activity imagery signals "not a swipe app"; explicit 18+ line sets expectation before data entry; language toggle respects Indian multilingual use.
  • Primary CTA: Get started → AUTH-03.
  • Secondary: Login → AUTH-05; language toggle.
  • Backend: None (content via remote config).
  • Data collected: Referral/deep-link parameters (host ID, Circle ID) stored to attribute the install and pre-select the Circle post-onboarding.
  • Next: AUTH-03, AUTH-05.
  • Error: Offline banner "You're offline. Connect to continue."
  • Edge cases: Arrived via Circle invite link → show a compact card "You're invited to Sunday Badminton · Koramangala" above the CTA; after onboarding land on that Circle.
  • Analytics: welcome_viewed (variant), get_started_tapped, login_tapped, language_changed.
  • Release: MVP · [CORE] (first conversion point)

PART 4 — AUTHENTICATION

Method decision: Phone number + OTP is the primary method (India-first; most users have one phone, phone verification also raises the cost of fake accounts). Google/Apple sign-in are optional secondary methods that still require phone verification afterward (Apple sign-in is required on iOS if any third-party social login is offered). Email is collected later, for recovery only. Passwords are not used in MVP.

OTP delivery in India (DLT registration, sender IDs, templates, cost per SMS, fallback to WhatsApp/voice) is an operational dependency. Register early; plan a vendor with fallback channels.

Auth state machine

[Logged out]
   │ enter phone
   ▼
[Phone submitted] ─ invalid format ─→ inline error
   │ send OTP
   ▼
[OTP sent] ─ wrong OTP (attempts<5) ─→ [OTP sent] +error
   │            ─ attempts=5 ─→ [Locked 15 min]
   │            ─ expired (10 min) ─→ [OTP expired] → resend
   │ correct OTP
   ▼
[Phone verified]
   ├─ phone exists, onboarding done ─→ [Logged in] → HOME
   ├─ phone exists, onboarding incomplete ─→ resume ONB
   ├─ phone exists, suspended ─→ SAFE-10
   ├─ phone exists, pending deletion ─→ SET-09 restore prompt
   └─ new phone ─→ [New account] → ONB-01

AUTH-03 — Phone entry (Sign up / Log in unified)

  • Purpose: Single entry: the backend decides new vs existing after OTP. This avoids "wrong screen" errors.
  • UI components: Country code selector (default +91), phone field (10 digits, numeric keypad, auto-format), primary Send OTP, "Continue with Google" / "Continue with Apple" (secondary buttons), consent checkbox-free line: "We'll text you a code. Message and data rates may apply."
  • Primary CTA: Send OTP.
  • User inputs: Phone number.
  • Backend: POST /auth/otp/request {phone, channel: sms}. Rate limits: 3 requests per phone per 15 min, 10 per device per day, IP throttling. Return {request_id, resend_after_sec} with a generic response regardless of whether the number exists (prevents account enumeration).
  • Data collected: Phone (hashed + encrypted), device ID, IP (for abuse only).
  • Next: AUTH-04.
  • Error state: Invalid number ("Enter a valid 10-digit mobile number"); rate limited ("Too many attempts. Try again in 12 minutes"); SMS provider down (auto-switch to backup provider; if all fail, show "We couldn't send a code. Try again or use email verification", email OTP fallback [V2]).
  • Edge cases: Number previously banned → generic "We can't create an account with this number" (no detail); virtual/VoIP numbers → flagged for stricter verification; dual-SIM users → clarify "the number this phone uses".
  • Analytics: signup_started, otp_requested, social_login_tapped.
  • Release: MVP.

AUTH-04 — OTP verification

  • UI components: 6 individual digit boxes with auto-advance; Android SMS auto-read (SMS Retriever API); iOS one-time-code autofill; countdown "Resend in 0:30"; "Wrong number? Edit" link; primary button appears only when 6 digits entered (auto-submit acceptable).
  • States: | State | UI | Recovery | |---|---|---| | Default | Boxes empty, timer running | — | | Wrong OTP | Boxes shake, red text "That code isn't right. 4 tries left." | Retype | | Attempts exhausted | "Too many tries. Try again in 15 minutes." Disable input | Wait; or contact support link | | Expired | "This code expired. Send a new one." | Resend | | Resend available | Timer done; "Resend code" (sms) + "Get code via call/WhatsApp" [V2] | Tap | | Resend limit hit | "You've reached the limit. Try again later." | Wait | | Network fail | Toast + retry, code preserved | Retry | | Success | Boxes turn green briefly; route by state machine | — |
  • Backend: POST /auth/otp/verify {request_id, code} → {access_token, refresh_token, account_state}. OTP: 6 digits, 10-min TTL, single-use, stored hashed.
  • Data collected: Verified phone (creates verifications record type=phone).
  • Edge cases: User switches apps to read SMS and returns (preserve state); OTP typed after resend (invalidate previous OTP on resend); clipboard auto-paste suggestion.
  • Analytics: otp_verified, otp_failed (reason), otp_resend_tapped.
  • Release: MVP.

AUTH-05 — Login (returning user)

Same UI as AUTH-03 with header "Welcome back." After OTP success, route by account_state. Adds "Log in on this device?" notification to other active devices [V2] (see Suspicious login below).

Existing vs new account

Handled after OTP by the server. If a user signs up with Google that maps to a phone already registered, prompt "This Google account is linked to a different LIGHT account. Verify the phone to link." Never merge accounts automatically.

AUTH-06 — Account recovery (lost phone number)

  • Purpose: User no longer has access to registered phone.
  • Flow: "Can't access this number?" → enter recovery email (if previously verified) → email magic link/OTP → verify new phone by OTP → old sessions revoked → security notification email. If no recovery email: submit support request with selfie liveness match to the account's verified photo (if photo-verified) → manual review, 48h SLA.
  • Abuse prevention: 24-hour cooldown before recovered account can send Connection requests or create Circles; email + push notice to all prior devices.
  • Release: MVP for email path; support-assisted for others.

Suspicious login & multi-device

  • Triggers: new device + new city/IP class; many failed OTPs; SIM-swap heuristics (rapid device change after phone re-registration).
  • Response: step-up challenge (email OTP or liveness selfie if photo-verified); push to other devices "New login from Mumbai. Not you? Log out everywhere."
  • Sessions screen (SET-06): list of devices, last active, "Log out of this device", "Log out of all devices". MVP: max 2 concurrent devices.
  • Token model: Access token 15 min; refresh token 30 days rotating; revoked on password/phone change, on "log out everywhere", and on ban.

5.2 Under-age handling

  • Minimum age: 18. (India's DPDP Act defines a child as under 18 and requires verifiable parental consent for processing children's data; avoiding minors removes that regime and much safety complexity.)
  • ONB-01 flow: DOB picker → if under 18: "LIGHT is for people 18 and over. We can't create an account for you." No continue button; no data stored beyond a short-lived device-level block flag (to prevent immediate retry with a different DOB). Do not reveal the block reason on retry (show the same message).
  • Age assurance layering: self-declared DOB (MVP) + photo-based age estimation at photo upload (flag/queue if estimated < 21 with a confidence threshold, to be tuned) + report-driven review. Underage account discovered → immediate suspension, data deletion, moderation log entry.
  • Adult-minor pairing: No product feature should ever match by age proximity below 18. Age preferences are bounded 18+.

5.3 Verification tiers (summary; details in Part 8)

Phone verified (mandatory) → Photo verified (selfie liveness vs profile photo) → Optional ID/student/workplace (V2).

5.4 Privacy controls at identity level

  • Hide exact age (show "20s")
  • Hide last name (never collected)
  • Hide workplace/college (never collected in MVP)
  • Hide from People discovery (Circle-only visibility)
  • Pseudonymous display name allowed
  • Profile photo visibility: Circle members / Connections only
  • Download/erase data (SET-08)

PART 6 — ONBOARDING (screen by screen)

Skippable steps get "Skip for now" and are re-prompted contextually later (e.g., availability prompt when joining a Circle).

ONB-01 — Age & basics

  • Purpose: Age gate; capture gender for eligibility filters.
  • UI: DOB picker (day/month/year wheels, not free text), gender chips (Woman / Man / Non-binary / Self-describe / Prefer not to say) with "Why we ask" info link: "Used only for women-only Circles and dating eligibility. Never shown unless you choose."
  • Action → Data → Backend → Next: Select DOB + gender → dob, gender → PUT /onboarding/basics (server computes age; rejects <18) → ONB-02, or ONB-01b block screen.
  • Edge: Users try multiple DOBs → device-level lock 24h after under-18 entry. Leap-year and future dates validated.
  • Analytics: age_gate_passed, age_gate_blocked (no DOB logged).
  • Release: MVP.

ONB-02 — What are you here for?

  • UI: Four large selectable cards (multi-select): Make friends, Meet someone (dating), Find people for activities, Not sure yet. Under each, one line of plain-language description. Below: "You can change this anytime. It's private."
  • Note on "Communities" and "Events" from your brief: These are places to do those things, not intents. Everyone gets communities/activities; the intent only governs how connections escalate.
  • Data: intent[]. Backend: PUT /onboarding/intent. Logic: Selecting Dating triggers an optional follow-up sheet: "Who would you be open to meeting?" (Women / Men / Everyone / Skip) plus optional orientation label. Data flagged high-sensitivity; used only for dating eligibility.
  • Next: ONB-03.
  • Edge: "Not sure" + others selected → treat as union; "Not sure" alone → prompt again after third completed Activity.
  • Analytics: intent_selected (values).
  • Release: MVP.

ONB-08 — Safety & privacy setup

  • Purpose: Make safe defaults visible and set the user's boundaries before they meet anyone.
  • UI: Single scrollable screen with pre-set toggles and plain-language explanations:

15.2 Escalation mechanics (CONN-01)

  1. After post-Activity feedback (EVT-04) or from a profile, A taps "I'd like to keep in touch" on B.
  2. A chooses why: Friends, Activity buddy, Dating (only shown if A's intent includes Dating and B's does; B's intent is not revealed to A — the server only offers the option when compatible).
  3. B receives a request card (not a message): "{A} from Sunday Badminton would like to connect." with Friends, Dating (only if eligible), Keep in Circle, No thanks. B never sees A's stated reason if declined.
  4. Result matrix:
A asks B's reply Outcome
Friends Friends / Activity buddy Friend connection
Friends Keep in Circle Stays in Circle; A gets soft "not now"
Friends No A sees nothing; cooldown 30 days before re-request
Dating Dating Dating connection (both explicit)
Dating Friends Friend connection (A receives "B would like to connect as friends" and can accept or decline)
Dating No/Keep in Circle Treated as "not now"
  1. Declines are private — A gets a neutral "no response yet" state for 7 days then "no longer available." No rejection notification.
  2. Request limits: 5 outgoing pending per user per week (raise with good standing); prevents spamming.

15.3 Scenarios

  • A — Friendship: Join Circle → attend 2 Activities → prompt → Friends request → accepted → 1:1 chat with suggested next Activity → friend badge.
  • B — Dating: Join Circle (intent includes Dating) → repeated interaction → Connect (Dating) → B (also Dating) accepts → private chat with date suggestions and safety card. If B is Friends-only: option converts gracefully to Friend.
  • C — Community only: Intent = Just activities → no escalation prompts; can receive Activity-buddy requests; can disable all 1:1 in settings.
  • D — Unsure: Escalation offers Friends/Activity buddy only; after the third completed Activity, ask "Anything you'd like to change about what you're looking for?"

PART 38 — DESIGN PRINCIPLES

  1. Context before contact. No one can contact a stranger; contact arises from shared Circles/Activities.
  2. Do not optimize for swipes, likes or time-in-app. Optimize for repeated positive interaction (MCR).
  3. Small groups beat big feeds. Default social unit is 4–8 people.
  4. Users control intent. Friends, Dating, Activities, Unsure — changeable, private, and never forced.
  5. Dating is an outcome, not the entry. Mutual escalation only.
  6. Safety is architecture, not a feature. Defaults protect the user; reporting is always one tap away.
  7. Privacy by default and by minimization. Collect only what changes a recommendation or a safety setting; approximate location only.
  8. Explain recommendations. Every card says why; no fake compatibility percentages.
  9. AI assists; it doesn't impersonate or manipulate. No AI-written messages sent on behalf of users; no AI "partners".
  10. Offline is optional and supported. Online-first paths for anxious users; public places, check-ins and help for offline.
  11. Rejection is gentle and private. No public "declined" states; no shame mechanics.
  12. No dark patterns. No FOMO notifications, hidden paywalls, or streaks.
  13. Hosts are partners. Give them tools, guidelines and accountability.
  14. Progress through repetition. Design for the second and third meeting, not the first swipe.
  15. Build for India. Low-end Android, multilingual, phone-first, culturally aware privacy, and regionally relevant interests.
